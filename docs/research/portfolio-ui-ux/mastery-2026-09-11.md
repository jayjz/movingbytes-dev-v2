## Three directions that deserve separate prototypes

### A — Observatory Ledger

First viewport: concise identity line, large two/three-line engineering thesis on the left, short constraints/background paragraph on the right, small Observer above it. The first project heading or pair is visible below the rule. Plain anchor navigation; typography is the primary visual.

Flagship: CipherLoop and TraceForge read as a connected pair through a real artifact boundary. Use three open columns—producer, durable files, evaluator—with an explicit source-derived connector; descriptions and source links are visible without opening anything. Verification/limits sit immediately below, in readable prose rather than tiny badges. Secondary work enters as a text index, with AetherForge first if evidence supports it.

Mobile: identity/Observer share a compact reserved row; thesis and intro follow. The flow becomes a vertical sequence with content in DOM reading order. One CSS separator system, no scroll-linked behavior. SVG is redundant with text. Complexity low; accessibility risk low. Risk: timid type/too many little metadata labels could recreate a drab README rather than an authored portfolio.

### B — Field Instrument

First viewport: smaller professional identity in a left reading rail; a large actual system schematic is the principal composition, with the thesis tied to a concrete boundary in it. This is spatial and artifact-led, unlike A's headline-led opening. Navigation is a compact document index aligned to the rail. Observer sits at the diagram margin as an instrument signature, with no speech or UI role.

Flagship: a horizontal workshop-like chain of evidence production, persisted artifacts, and independent inspection, each with clear input/output labels. Open whitespace and fine rules define the components; no artificial dashboard telemetry. A contrasting technical plate for AetherForge can later show accept/reject paths. Proof is attached to the corresponding boundary, not a separate metric strip.

Mobile: title/identity, plain-language system question, stacked schematic, then links. No horizontal panning. Optional focus/hover emphasis follows a single relationship, never hides facts. Complexity medium: connector reflow and label lengths need care. Risk: small labels, too much mono, or insufficient introduction would turn the hero into documentation and make the visitor decode it.

### C — Systems Narrative

First viewport: an unusually large, short thesis plus small identity and a compact work index; maximum contrast and negative space. The next scene begins visibly below it. Navigation remains ordinary anchors. Observer is anchored near a section boundary, not centered as a hero illustration.

Flagship: CipherLoop first, a broad bone evidence plate for the artifact handoff, then TraceForge as an independent next act. Each scene answers one question with a short heading and distinct diagram scale. Source/evidence links repeat at each relevant scene. Motion is one brief, optional state reveal; content is never hidden by default or locked to scroll.

Mobile: scenes become ordinary consecutive articles without pinned panels; the diagram and limits are kept adjacent. Static/reduced-motion page must carry the full narrative. Complexity medium/high; accessibility risk medium if choreography creeps in. Risk: the evidence pair arrives too late and a sophisticated viewer must scroll too far to assess the work. Penalize this heavily in scoring rather than rewarding atmosphere alone.

Provisional preference: A has the best fit for this bounded slice. B is the strongest genuinely different challenger if a clear current artifact flow supports it. C is useful as a comparison of pacing, but must earn its larger vertical cost. Final scoring must follow actual prototype renders.

## Observer assessment

Inspected existing SVG, CSS, JS, checkpoint/placement documents and saved final placement screenshots at 1440/390. Local branch artwork is the same object inspected from `design/observer-character` before root restored that baseline. This is a review of existing captures/source, not a fresh interaction test.

Keep the identity: chamfered bone housing, carry loop, unequal optical eyes, orange side pivot, and grounded folding stand. It looks like an authored workshop instrument and remains legible when small. Regeneration would sacrifice established personality without solving a problem.

Existing implementation is appropriately bounded: eye translations limited to 7/4 SVG units horizontally and 4.55/2.6 vertically, head tilt 1.6 degrees, animation frames stop after settling, reduced motion/visibility guards, and a pause control for periodic blinking. Existing checkpoint reports Chromium checks; those do not automatically certify a new production mount.

Main issue is placement cost. Existing 390px final placement reserves a 96×140 mount and the CipherLoop heading begins about y825. The 1440px mount reserves a 144px character plus caption/control. The character competes little, but its controls/caption add reading noise and postpone proof.

Recommended production scope: preserve the SVG; use a reserved compact identity-line perch around 112px desktop and 72–80px mobile, with explicit intrinsic dimensions. Keep it out of headline/introduction measures. Event-driven bounded gaze is enough personality for the initial slice; static fallback should be visually complete. If periodic idle blink remains, retain its pause control. Do not add a greeting button merely to demonstrate interactivity. Keep critical navigation independent of the Observer. New integration must test missing script, reduced motion, no JS, focus, and text enlargement.

Do not add section-state behavior now unless it clearly reinforces the producer→artifact→evaluator relationship without implying live telemetry. A small static signature already meets the identity goal.
