Curiosity

Model behavior · TEMPER

When should a model decline to answer?

A confident answer can still be wrong. I’m interested in what happens when a system can abstain or pass a decision on.

The inspected TEMPER source measures coverage, errors among accepted predictions, and calibration. Those are tools for asking the question; they don’t establish that a model knows when to stop.

  • How many decisions remain when the confidence threshold rises?
  • How often are the accepted decisions wrong?
  • Does reported confidence match observed correctness?

Source inspected at 0747730. Tests and experiments not rerun here; no cost or reliability advantage established.

A note from field work

Check what reached the component.

A stopped unit and a missing signal can look like the same failure from the outside. I want to know what the component received, what it did, and what the next person was told. I ask the same questions at a software boundary.

How I think

Working principles

A few habits I bring to technical work. Current questions ↓

  1. Make authority explicit.

    Separate what a system proposes from what it is allowed to do.

  2. Keep the evidence.

    Decisions and outcomes should remain understandable outside the process that produced them.

  3. Learn from the failure.

    Leave enough context to understand what happened and decide what comes next.

  4. Check the handoff.

    Make consequential exchanges explicit without unnecessarily constraining intelligence inside the boundary.
